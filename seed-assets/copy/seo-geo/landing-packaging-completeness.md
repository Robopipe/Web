## What a complete pack means

A complete pack holds everything it should and nothing extra. Packaging completeness inspection therefore checks four things on every pack:

- **All parts in place.** No ingredient is missing from the assembled product and none is swapped.
- **Right count.** The pack holds exactly as many pieces as it should.
- **Nothing extra.** No foreign object got into the pack.
- **Intact pack.** The seal of the pack is tight.

You can combine the checks. One camera checks all of them on every pack at once.

## Fill inspection

For containers and blisters, the task is fill inspection. The camera sees the fill level and counts the pieces in the container. A badly filled container does not reach your customer. The [part counting with a camera](/en/camera-part-counting) page has more about counting.

## Camera packaging inspection on the line

The camera over the belt checks every pack, not a sample. Operators see the verdict on a display on the floor, and an image of every check stays for the audit.

The model learns from images of your packs. This is machine vision, and the [guide to machine vision](/en/blog/what-is-a-vision-system-a-plain-english-guide-to-machine-vision-in-manufacturing) gives an introduction. The [camera quality inspection](/en/camera-quality-inspection) page describes the camera, the control unit and the rollout. Defects of the product itself are a task for [defect detection on the production line](/en/defect-detection).
