## What label inspection checks

Label inspection with a camera checks four things on every piece:

- **Label present.** The camera marks a piece without a label as NOK.
- **Position and orientation.** The label must sit in the right place and must not be turned.
- **Legible batch code.** The camera checks that the batch code can be read.
- **Best-before date.** The camera reads the best-before date from the label.

Operators see the verdict on a display on the floor. An image of every check stays in the archive, so you can find it for an audit.

## Reading the best-before date

Reading the best-before date runs on every piece right on the line, like the other label checks. The camera reads the date from the label and the result goes to the operator display with the other checks.

## Print inspection

Print inspection is the same task. The camera checks that the print on the pack is in place and legible. The model learns from images of your correct and faulty pieces, so nobody writes rules for each kind of label.

Label inspection is part of machine vision. The [guide to machine vision](/en/blog/what-is-a-vision-system-a-plain-english-guide-to-machine-vision-in-manufacturing) gives an introduction to the field.
